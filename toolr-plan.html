Toolr Phase 1 · Architecture & MVP definition

Borrow the drill from the person 300 metres away.

An Android-first app for lending and renting tools between neighbours. Two buttons on the home screen. Photograph a tool and it lists itself in about thirty seconds. Say what you need in plain language and the app works out what tool that is. Everything below exists to protect those two moments.

Product plan

The product is two sentences long: I need a drill → someone nearby has one → borrow it. Every feature that does not serve that sentence is a feature that makes the app feel like equipment-management software.

Who it is for

Dana, 34
Borrower — primary

Rents in Florentin. Wants to hang a TV. Owns a screwdriver. Will not buy a ₪400 hammer drill for one hole; currently asks the building WhatsApp group and hopes.

Wins when she gets a drill within a 15-minute walk, today, with no awkward negotiation.

Yossi, 58
Owner — primary

Forty tools, uses six. Likes helping neighbours, resents the clutter, distrusts apps that want his ID and bank details.

Wins when listing takes under a minute and he can decline without feeling rude.

Adi, 41
Owner — side income

Bought a pressure washer and a tile cutter for a renovation. Wants them to pay for themselves. Cares about damage and about who is borrowing.

Wins on predictable small income and confidence the tool comes back.

Michal, 67
Both — accessibility case

Mid-range Android, small text feels small, wants to lend her late husband's tools rather than throw them out. Needs large targets and plain Hebrew.

Wins when she never has to ask her son for help.

Anti-persona

The professional rental shop. Commercial accounts are excluded from the MVP on purpose — they change the tone from neighbour to vendor, and their inventory would drown out the free listings that make the product worth opening.

What success looks like in the pilot

Four numbers, and one diagnostic:

  • Listing completion rate and median time to list — target ≥70%, ≤60 seconds.
  • Search → borrow request — target ≥8% of searches that return a result.
  • Request → completed exchange — target ≥40%.
  • Repeat rate — borrowers who borrow again within 30 days. The only honest PMF signal.
  • Diagnostic: AI acceptance rate — accepted as-is vs corrected vs rejected. This tells us whether the thirty-second promise is real.
02

Technical plan

Each decision below is stated with the alternative it beat. The weighting throughout: solo builder, cost-sensitive, Android-first, Israel-first.

Mobile
React Native + Expo, TypeScript

One language from screen to server: Edge Functions are Deno/TypeScript, and types generated from the Postgres schema are imported by both. EAS builds the APK in the cloud, so there is no Android Studio on the critical path. Camera, location, notifications, localization and image manipulation are all first-party modules. expo-updates ships a JS fix without a Play review — worth a great deal in the first weeks of a pilot.

Instead of Flutter — genuinely better for pixel-identical custom UI, but it splits the stack into Dart and TypeScript and has no equally clean OTA story. And native Kotlin, which is the best Android result and the worst time-to-MVP.

Backend
Supabase

This is really a geospatial decision. The core query is “active tools within R metres, matching these types, ranked” — one indexed ST_DWithin in PostGIS. Firestore has no native geo query; the standard workaround is geohash prefix ranges fanned across nine bounding-box queries and re-filtered on device, and it composes badly with the price, availability and category filters we need. Row Level Security then expresses the privacy rule as a database policy rather than a thing every code path must remember.

Instead of Firebase — better push and Google Sign-In ergonomics, but Cloud Functions require the paid Blaze plan just to exist, and the geo story is a workaround. We cover the gap with Expo Push, Sentry and PostHog.

Identity
Guest · email + password · Google

Four ways in, one ladder. Guest is automatic on first open — no tap, no form — and is a real Supabase anonymous session. Member is email + password or Google One Tap. Verified (phone, then ID) comes in V1.1 and is what unlocks higher-value tools once deposits exist. Facebook and Apple sit behind one signInWith(provider) function and one button row.

Guests browse; they do not borrow. A borrow request is the moment a neighbour hands over physical property. If the person on the other side is unaccountable, the owner carries all the risk — and the predictable outcome isn't happy guests, it's owners switching guest requests off after the first tool that doesn't come back. Requiring an account at that exact moment costs one tap and buys the owner a name, a verified email, and a rating history to build on.

Why a session and not just the anon key Identity linking keeps the same user id through the upgrade — so a guest who hearted four drills finds four hearted drills after signing in, with no migration step. It also gives each guest a rate-limit bucket for AI search, and puts the is_anonymous claim in the JWT so is_member() can gate every write policy in Postgres rather than in each screen.

Passwords
Not a toggle — budget real work for it

Custom SMTP is mandatory from day one. Supabase's built-in sender is capped at 2 messages per hour and only delivers to pre-authorised team addresses, with no delivery SLA. It physically cannot send a user a password reset. Resend's free tier covers the pilot; verify SPF and DKIM or the mail lands in spam and people conclude the app is broken.

Leaked-password protection on (HaveIBeenPwned), minimum length 8, a strength meter that advises rather than blocks — no forced symbol-and-digit rules, which reliably produce Passw0rd! and nothing else. The reset form returns an identical response whether or not the address exists, so it isn't an account-enumeration oracle.

The collision everyone hits Dana signs up with a password, then three weeks later taps Continue with Google on the same address. Decision: auto-link, but only when both sides have a verified email. Auto-linking on an unverified address is exactly how account-takeover-by-signup works.

Maps
react-native-maps, Google provider

Correct Hebrew labelling out of the box, which matters more for an Israeli launch than styling flexibility. Pins are clustered; a tool page draws a circle, not a pin. Reverse geocoding for the neighbourhood label runs on-device, once, at listing time — so we pay for no geocoding at all.

Watch this Google restructured Maps pricing in 2025; mobile map loads are now billed SKUs with a monthly free threshold rather than unlimited-free. MVP volumes sit inside it, but set a quota cap and a billing alert on day one. All map usage sits behind one component, so MapLibre is a contained swap.

Push
Expo Push Service → FCM v1

Free, and it abstracts the FCM setup. Sending happens only server-side. Every notification is also a row in notifications, rendered in an in-app inbox — push is a convenience, the inbox is the truth. That is the mitigation for OEM battery managers that silently drop messages. Seven transactional types in V1, per-type toggles from day one, no marketing pushes.

AI
Gemini Flash-Lite, server-side only

The cheapest capable multimodal tier, with schema-constrained JSON output. A 1024 px photo costs a fraction of an agora to identify. The same function handles natural-language query interpretation, cached by query hash so popular searches cost nothing.

Instead of Cloud Vision label detection, which returns “tool / hardware / machine”, and Vision Product Search, which requires you to build the catalogue — the actual hard part. There is no public Google Lens API; the brief was right to flag it. Anything advertised as one is a scraper.

Analytics
PostHog (EU) + Sentry

Both free at this scale. Autocapture off — events are instrumented deliberately, about twenty-five of them. No PII in properties: the user is a Supabase UUID, never an email, name, phone or coordinate. Sentry's beforeSend scrubs coordinates and message bodies.

Payments
None in V1 — and a provider interface, not a Stripe integration

Google Play's Payments policy explicitly excludes “purchases or rentals of physical goods” from the Play Billing requirement, so a peer-to-peer tool rental owes Google nothing. Play is not the obstacle. Israel is: Stripe does not currently list Israel among its supported countries, which rules out Stripe Connect — the default marketplace answer — for an Israeli platform.

So the schema carries payment_provider, payment_status, provider_ref, platform_fee_agorot and deposit_agorot, all inert, behind a PaymentProvider interface whose only V1 implementation records intent and moves no money. Candidates for later — Tranzila, Cardcom, Grow/Meshulam, PayPlus, Rapyd — all need direct diligence on payouts to individuals, which is the hard part.

In the product a listing is FREE, or “₪X/day, paid directly to the owner”. In Israel that means cash or Bit, which everyone already has. This is not a compromise: it removes KYC, chargebacks, tax reporting and dispute liability from the MVP entirely.

Information architecture

Five tabs with a raised centre button. The two big buttons the brief asks for live on Home, because that is the path for someone using the app for the third time in their life. The tab bar is the escape hatch for someone using it for the thirtieth. There is no hamburger menu — and no sign-in screen standing in front of any of it.

The screens that carry the product

Home

App name small. A location chip reading “Florentin · change”. A search field placeholder, “What do you need?”. Then two full-width buttons at least 64 dp tall — 🔍 I need a tool and ➕ I have a tool — and a horizontal Near you carousel. Nothing else. No feed, no promos, no stats.

Results

A list row is photo, title, 📍 350 m, FREE or ₪15/day, ★ 4.9, heart. Filters sit in a quiet bar, visually secondary. When a query was interpreted by AI, a dismissible chip says so — “Showing hammer drills and rotary hammers for ‘hole in a concrete wall’” — with Search the exact words instead. Silent query rewriting is the fastest way to make search feel broken.

The listing flow

Camera opens immediately; no form comes first. Identification runs for at most 12 seconds against a scan overlay, then the confirmation screen, then a single details screen with exactly two decisions — FREE / RENT ₪__ per day and Available now / Choose dates / Ask me — with everything else collapsed under Add more details. Then LIST TOOL.

Pickup

Reachable only once a request is accepted. This is the screen where the exact address appears, and the only one that has it.

Signing in

Never a full-screen gate. A bottom sheet rises over whatever the person was doing, and its headline names the action rather than the ceremony: “Sign in to ask Yossi for the drill”, not “Create an account”. Continue with Google, a divider, an email field. Dismissing it leaves them a guest with everything they had; completing it returns them to the exact borrow request they had half-composed. Five screens in total — sign-in, sign-up, check-your-email, forgot-password, reset-password — plus a Sign-in & security settings screen that shows what is linked and lets someone add a password to a Google account or the reverse.

Empty states are a feature here, not a fallback

No tools nearby: “Nothing nearby yet. Try a wider radius — or post what you're looking for and we'll let nearby owners know.” with Widen to 3 km and Post a request. In a marketplace this thin, the empty state is the growth mechanism: it converts unmet demand into a broadcast that creates supply.

No listings: “Your toolbox is looking lonely. Add your first tool.”

Rules that keep it consistent

  • Loading is skeletons matching the final layout — never a spinner on a full screen, and nothing at all under 400 ms so there is no flash.
  • Permission denial is never a dead end. Location denied → manual area picker. Camera denied → gallery. Notifications denied → the inbox still works.
  • Offline keeps cached results readable and queues writes as pending, retried on reconnect.
  • AI failure always falls through to the manual picker within 12 seconds. The listing flow can never be blocked by the AI — that is the difference between a delightful shortcut and a dependency.
  • RTL uses only logical properties — marginStart, never marginLeft — and every directional icon goes through one mirroring helper.

Database schema

24 tables, 18 enums, 39 RLS policies, 10 functions. The full migration is in sql/001_initial_schema.sql; what follows is why it is shaped this way.

The decisions that matter

Guests are rows in auth.users and nowhere else

A guest has an auth.users row with is_anonymous = true and deliberately no profiles row — they cannot list, borrow, message or rate, so a profile for them would be junk. provision_member() creates it at exactly two moments: a normal sign-up, and the instant a guest upgrades. Two foreign keys point at auth.users rather than profiles, both deliberately: favorites, so a guest's hearts survive the upgrade untouched, and ai_usage_quota, so guests get their own rate-limit bucket rather than an IP bucket that punishes everyone behind one café's Wi-Fi.

verification_level only ever moves upward. Confirming an email must never downgrade someone who has already verified a phone — an easy bug to write and an infuriating one to receive.

The location split is the architecture

Postgres RLS is row-level, not column-level — so a public read policy on tools would expose every column on that row, coordinates included. Putting the exact point in its own table is the only way to say “this row is public, that coordinate is not” and have the database enforce it rather than the application remember it.

Money is bigint agorot

₪15/day is 1500. Never a float, never a decimal type. Every money row also carries currency char(3), so expanding beyond ILS is a configuration change rather than a migration.

Requests and transactions are separate tables

borrow_requests is the negotiation; transactions is the commitment, created only on acceptance. Collapsing them into one row with a bigger enum sounds simpler until you need to expire unanswered requests without touching real transactions, or count requests sent separately from exchanges completed.

Ratings are double-blind

A rating publishes when both sides have rated, or after seven days. Without that window the first rater sets the tone and the second rates retaliatorily. Reputation counters only move when a rating publishes — and smoothed_rating() applies a Bayesian prior so one 5-star review does not outrank forty 4.8s. In a young marketplace where most people have one or two reviews, that single function is the difference between a ranking that means something and one that doesn't.

The AI tables are the evaluation dataset

ai_identification_results stores the raw response, the validated parse, the confidence — and what the user did about it. That last column is the product's most valuable telemetry: it is the direct measurement of whether the thirty-second promise holds.

Verified, not asserted

Ran against Postgres 16 + PostGIS

The migration was applied to a live database and its guarantees exercised. These assertions belong in CI — they are what stops a future migration from quietly publishing everyone's home address.

AI tool identification

The governing rule

The AI is a shortcut, never an authority. It fills the form faster than the person could. The person always approves. The app must work identically, only slower, when the AI is wrong, slow, or down.

Pipeline

PHONE   capture → resize to 1024 px, q0.70, strip EXIF including GPS
        → upload to private bucket ai-temp/
        → POST /functions/v1/identify-tool  + user JWT
           │

        ├ consume_ai_quota('identify', 25)         429 if over budget

Every arrow has a timeout and a fallback. The client budget is 12 seconds; past that the user lands on the manual picker with their photo already attached.

The confidence policy

This runs server-side, so the client cannot be tricked into displaying an overconfident claim. Before anything is returned: 

if (model_confidence < 0.70) model = null   do not state a model we're unsure of
if (brand_confidence < 0.60) brand = null
Hightype ≥ 0.80

“We think this is: Bosch GSB 18V-55 Cordless Combi Drill”

One candidate. YES / NOT QUITE.

Medium0.50 – 0.79

“Which looks closest?”

Two to four ranked options, each tagged Possible match, plus None of these.

Low< 0.50

“We couldn't tell what this is. What kind of tool is it?”

Candidates suppressed entirely. Straight to the category grid.

Not a toolis_tool: false

“That doesn't look like a tool. Try another photo?”

With Add it manually beside it. This check also blocks obvious junk uploads.

Three phrases are shown to users, consistently, and never a percentage: Likely · Possible match · Model uncertain. “83% confident” reads as precision the model does not have; people either over-trust it or find it baffling. “Just call it a generic cordless drill” appears on every variant including the high-confidence one — it is often the honest answer and always the fastest.

Listings carry the provenance: is_model_confirmed is true only when a human confirmed a specific model, and unconfirmed models render with the same uncertainty treatment on the borrower's screen that the owner saw. That symmetry is the whole point.

Natural language, and where it goes next

"I need to make a hole in a concrete wall"
   ↓ sha256 cache hit?  →  return, 0 tokens, ~5 ms
   ↓ miss → Gemini, text only, JSON schema
{ "tool_types": ["rotary-hammer", "hammer-drill", "cordless-drill"],
  "keywords":   ["concrete", "masonry", "SDS"],
  "explanation_en": "hammer drills and rotary hammers" }
   ↓

Popular queries are interpreted once, ever. The interpretation is always shown and always reversible. If the function fails or the user is offline it degrades to keyword search — slightly worse results, zero breakage. Voice input is native on-device speech-to-text, including Hebrew, and enters this same path: voice is an input method, not a separate feature.

Phase 3 — the feature this architecture is really for

“I want to hang a TV on a concrete wall” → a structured kit: hammer drill (required), 8 mm masonry bit (required), spirit level (required), stud detector (recommended, avoid drilling into wiring), screwdriver — then one nearby search per item and a single answer: “3 of 5 available within 1 km.”

Nothing in the MVP schema has to change for this. interpret-query already returns a list of tool types and search_tools_nearby already accepts a text[] of them. What it needs is a curated task→kit knowledge base, because a model left alone will confidently invent drill-bit diameters. That is why it is Phase 3.

Worth noting and not worth building yet: “you'll also need 8 mm wall plugs” is the most natural local-hardware-shop moment this product will ever have.

06

Security & privacy

Toolr asks people to publish, on a map, the approximate location of a valuable object inside their home. Getting this wrong is not a bug. It is a burglary tool.

Why the fuzz is deterministic

This is the detail most implementations get wrong. If the published point re-randomises on every read, an attacker who reads the listing a few dozen times can average the noise away and recover the true location. So the offset is derived once, from the tool id plus a server-side salt, and never re-rolled. For the same reason displayed distances are rounded to 50 m: precise distances from three vantage points trilaterate a position, rounded ones do not.

And one thing that quietly undoes all of it if you forget: GPS EXIF is stripped from every photo on the device before upload. A listing photo taken in a living room otherwise carries the exact coordinates in its metadata.

Whether the platform becomes a party to the rental once money moves, and what that means for Israeli consumer-protection and tax rules. This is the strongest argument for keeping V1 free-or-cash-only: it defers the question until there is a product worth answering it for.
